---
title: "misogyny"
date: "2026-07-01"
---

it feels like the default mental settings of a man could possibly be an incel and a pervert. that's some kind of weird brain-wiring, or is it just the hormones doing their unchecked damage? are women curious like this too since just the touch of puberty — that same low-level suspicion of the opposite, that same instinctive reduction? i don't know. but i suspect the answer is no, or at least not in the same structural way, because misogyny isn't just a feeling a few unfortunate men happen to have. it's load-bearing. it holds something up. it's been built into the architecture of most civilisations so early and so quietly that a lot of men never notice they're standing inside it.

that same architecture is what links misogyny to homophobia, and homophobia is — obviously, almost definitionally — a male concept. to be in the pack. to prove, at every possible moment, that you are not soft, not feminine, not one of *them*. i was homophobic myself from around class 9th, right when the world was tipping into what everyone started calling the age of the "woke." and looking back, my homophobia had almost nothing to do with gay men. it had everything to do with the performance of being a man, and the terror of being seen as failing that performance. simone de beauvoir wrote that "one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman." i'd extend that a little: one is not born, but rather is drilled into being, a man. the drilling happens early and it is not gentle. don't cry. don't sit like that. don't like that thing. don't be soft. every don't is a small chisel taking something off. and what's left when enough has been chiselled away is someone who has learned to mistake his own mutilation for strength.

i want to be clear that i am not a fan of making sexuality a whole personality or using it as some permanent victim card — that flattens a human being in a different direction. but there is a difference between not reducing yourself to a label and pretending the thing the label points to doesn't cost you anything in this world. the cost is real. i just don't think performing your wound constantly is the same as healing it.

the hatred toward women — because let's call it what it is — is something a male child absorbs almost before he has language for it. every house where the man's voice is the final word, every room where a woman's opinion is heard and then gently set aside, every joke about wives, every film where the woman is furniture the plot moves around — all of it goes in. it doesn't announce itself. it just becomes what normal looks like. bell hooks, who wrote about this with a clarity that still feels like cold water on the face, said that "the first act of violence that patriarchy demands of males is not violence toward women. it is violence toward themselves." the required shutdown of tenderness. the mandatory eviction of the feminine from the self. and then, once you've done that to yourself, the woman in front of you stops being a full person — she becomes the mirror of everything you've suppressed, and people tend to either worship or resent the mirror.

if given a chance i would beg — and i say that with conviction — for every man to spend real time with women. not to flirt, not to perform, not to be seen performing respect. just to sit in a room and actually listen. you will see the world differently. full stop. to see a woman as a person with her own interior life, her own private architecture of thought and grief and humour, is genuinely disorienting if you've never let yourself do it before, and that disorientation is the point. that's the feeling of a wall coming down.

it is genuinely absurd — the more i think about it the more absurd it gets — that women are still primarily perceived through their bodies. as though the body is the headline and everything else is fine print nobody reads. it is possibly the least of what they are, and the world keeps treating it like the most. virginia woolf wrote that "a woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction." the room is the metaphor. space to think, space to be, space that belongs to you and isn't contingent on someone else's permission or patience. most women in most of human history have been denied that room in ways so thorough and so normalised that entire libraries of female thought simply don't exist because the conditions for it never existed. we are running the experiment of civilisation with half the brain tied behind its back and then wondering why things feel stuck.

i am, and i mean this without irony or performance, deeply grateful to have had a mother so proud of her womanhood that she put it in my ear at a very young age — that a man without the touch of the intricate feminine is drawn into someone without gravity. unmoored. just mass moving through space with no real pull to anything. i think about that a lot. because gravity is what makes things orbit each other. what makes one thing matter to another. what makes you stay.

and i think that's what misogyny ultimately takes from the man himself — not just the woman he flattens, but his own capacity to be genuinely moved by another person. you can't reduce someone to an object and then be surprised when your own life starts to feel hollow. the reduced and the reducer both lose something. one of them just doesn't notice.

i've sat with all of this long enough now to arrive at something that sounds strange but feels completely true to me: if there are other lives after this one, i would want to come back as a woman in every single one of them. not because it is easier — it obviously isn't, not by a long stretch — but because everything i have seen, in my own house, in the women who have sat across from me and actually let me see them, tells me that to move through this world as a woman requires a kind of depth that most men are never asked to develop and therefore never do. the patience. the reading of rooms. the carrying of things nobody officially asked you to carry. the love that doesn't announce itself. that's not weakness dressed up as something else. that is, i think, the closest thing to actual strength that i have ever witnessed up close. and i would want to know what that feels like, from the inside, every time.
